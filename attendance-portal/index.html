University exam regulations

The 75% attendance rule, in plain words

The standard rule followed by universities: you must attend at least 75% of classes in every single subject to be allowed to sit the final exam.

75% – 100%

✅ Exam eligible

You meet the minimum attendance requirement in the subject. Sit back — but keep an eye on your buffer: the portal tells you exactly how many classes you can still miss.

65% – 74.9%

⚠️ At risk — condonation case

Below the requirement but within the condonation window. Your name goes to the attendance committee, which may allow you to sit the exam for valid reasons (medical, university duty) — at its discretion.

Below 65%

⛔ Not eligible — detained

As per university rules you are detained from the exam in that subject. Meet your academic advisor immediately; the portal shows exactly how many classes in a row you must attend to recover.

Thresholds (75% / 65%) are configured by the exam cell and can differ per university.

Questions students actually ask

FAQ

Do I need an account or password?

No. Your roll number is already public within the university (it's on the notice board), so the portal uses it directly. Nothing beyond your name, roll number and attendance is ever shown.

Can other people see my attendance?

Yes — the same people who could read the notice board. The exam cell can switch off public checking entirely from the admin settings if policy changes.

The percentage looks wrong. What do I do?

Print your report (button on the check page) and take it to the exam cell or your teacher. Only the admin can change attendance records — students can never edit anything.

What does "condonation" mean?

If you land between 65% and 75%, you are not automatically barred. The attendance committee reviews your reason (medical, sports, university duty) and can condone the shortage. Below 65% there is no condonation.

Is this real university software?

It's a course-project demo with sample data. The rules it applies (75% pass mark, 65% condonation floor) mirror standard university ordinances, and all data is managed by one admin account.
